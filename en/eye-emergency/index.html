Eye emergency

Eye injury or sudden loss of vision — what to do right now

If a chemical has gone in the eye, rinse with clean water for 15 minutes FIRST, then call.

Call now 0300-0000000

Chemicals, acid, lime or cleaning fluid in the eye

Right now, immediately, rinse the eye with clean water for at least 15 minutes without stopping. Don't call first — rinse first. Hold the eye open and let the water run. Call the moment you have finished. Every minute counts.

A blow to the eye

Do not rub it and do not press on it. Do not bandage anything tightly over it. Do not eat or drink — if surgery is needed the stomach must be empty. Call immediately.

Something embedded in the eye

Do not try to remove it. Do not rub the eye. If you can, tape a clean paper cup upside down over the eye so nothing presses on it. Call immediately.

Sudden loss of vision with no pain

This is the most urgent situation of all. Do not wait to see if it clears. Call now.

Dust, sand or a small particle

Don't rub. Rinse with clean water. If the grittiness continues, the eye turns red or your vision changes, call.

If the clinic is closed

Do not wait. Delay causes harm in an eye emergency. Go straight to:

  • Local 24-hour hospital — name and address to be confirmed
  • Referral hospital — name, city and distance to be confirmed
  • Emergency number — to be confirmed

Contact us immediately if

  • Any chemical has entered the eye
  • The eye has taken a blow
  • Something is stuck in the eye
  • Vision has suddenly dropped or gone
  • There is severe eye pain
  • You suddenly see many black floaters or flashes of light
  • There is swelling around the eye with fever
