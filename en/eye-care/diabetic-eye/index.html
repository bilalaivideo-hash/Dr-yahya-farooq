Diabetes and your eyes — screening and treatment

Diabetes damages the fine blood vessels at the back of the eye. The dangerous part is that early on there is no pain and your sight seems fine — the damage happens quietly. That is why anyone with diabetes should have the retina checked at least once a year, even with perfect vision.

How diabetes damages the eye

Sustained high blood sugar weakens the fine vessels of the retina. They begin to leak, swelling develops, and eventually fragile new vessels can form and bleed.

The important point

Good vision does not mean a healthy retina. Damage begins years before sight is affected.

Who is most at risk

  • Diabetes of several years' standing
  • Sugar that is not well controlled
  • High blood pressure as well
  • Kidney problems
  • Diabetes that developed during pregnancy

When symptoms appear

Often very late. By the time you notice blurring, floaters or dark patches, the damage is usually well advanced. Don't wait for symptoms.

What the screening involves

Drops are used to widen the pupil and the retina is examined in detail. Vision may stay blurred for a few hours afterwards, so don't drive yourself — bring someone with you.

Treatment

The first and most effective treatment is controlling blood sugar and blood pressure. Where the retina has already changed, laser or further treatment is recommended according to the findings, and you are referred to a larger centre if that is what you need.

How often to come back

Once a year if the retina is clear. Sooner if changes are found.
