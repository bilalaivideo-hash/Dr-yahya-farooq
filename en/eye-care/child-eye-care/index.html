Children's eye care

Children don't complain — they assume everyone sees the way they do. So a parent's attention is the real screening. If your child holds books or a phone very close, sits right up against the television, screws up their eyes, tilts their head to look at things, or struggles to read the board at school, have their eyes checked. Squint and lazy eye respond far better the earlier treatment starts.

Signs to watch for

  • Holding books or a phone very close
  • Sitting right up against the television
  • Screwing up their eyes
  • Tilting the head to look at things
  • One eye drifting in or out
  • Frequent eye rubbing
  • Trouble reading the board at school
  • A sudden drop in interest in schoolwork

Squint

The eyes don't point in the same direction — one drifts inward or outward. It isn't only cosmetic: left untreated, sight in the turned eye gradually weakens.

Some children are corrected with glasses, some with patching or exercises, and some need surgery.

Lazy eye

One eye sees less well than the other because the brain has started favouring the stronger one. Glasses, patching and exercises can improve it — but the treatment only works well in early childhood.

Age matters

The earlier lazy eye is treated, the better. Past a certain age, vision becomes very difficult to improve. If you have any doubt, don't wait.

How children are examined

Examining a child is different from examining an adult. Picture charts are used for younger children, and drops are often needed to find the accurate prescription. Vision stays blurred for a few hours after the drops.

A check before school

One eye check before a child starts school is the simplest and most useful step you can take. A great many weak-sighted children are picked up for the first time at exactly that examination.
