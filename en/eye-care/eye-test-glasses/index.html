Eye test and glasses prescription

A sight test takes a short time. It checks distance and near vision, includes an internal examination of the eye, and produces an accurate prescription if you need one. If something else is behind the blurring — a cataract, or the effect of diabetes — that shows up in the same examination.

Signs your sight has changed

  • Distant things look blurred
  • Holding a book further away to read
  • Screwing up your eyes to focus
  • Headaches after reading
  • Difficulty driving at night
  • Tired, heavy eyes

What the test involves

Distance vision is checked on a chart, then near vision. Different lenses are then tried to find the prescription that gives you the clearest sight. Finally the eye is examined internally at the slit lamp.

The prescription is written out during the visit. You can have the glasses made wherever you prefer.

Testing children

Children don't complain — they assume everyone sees the way they do. If your child holds books very close, sits right up against the television, or struggles to read the board at school, have their eyes checked.

How often to recheck

Adults, usually once a year. Children more often, because their sight changes. Anyone with diabetes should be checked every year even if their vision seems fine.
