Glaucoma — testing and treatment

Glaucoma is raised pressure inside the eye that slowly damages the optic nerve. The critical point: early on there is no pain and your sight seems normal, and vision once lost does not come back. That makes timely testing everything. Risk is higher over forty, or if glaucoma runs in your family.

Cataract or glaucoma — the difference

Both are commonly called “motia” in everyday speech, but they are entirely different conditions, and the confusion delays treatment.

  • Cataract — the lens clouds over. Surgery restores sight.
  • Glaucoma — pressure damages the optic nerve. Lost vision does not return, but remaining sight can be protected.

Why timing matters

With a cataract you can wait. With glaucoma, waiting means permanent loss.

Who is most at risk

  • Over forty
  • A family history of glaucoma
  • Diabetes or high blood pressure
  • A previous eye injury
  • Long-term steroid drops or medication
  • A very high prescription

When symptoms appear

In the common form, nothing is felt early on. Damage begins at the edges of vision, and people usually notice only once a good deal of sight has gone.

Another form causes sudden severe pain, a red eye, headache, nausea and haloes around lights. That is an emergency — contact us immediately.

How it is tested

Eye pressure is measured, the optic nerve is examined at the slit lamp, and a peripheral vision test is done where needed. The testing is not uncomfortable.

Treatment

Most patients are treated with drops that keep the pressure down. They must be used daily, on time, and without gaps — missing them lets the damage continue. Laser or surgery is needed in some cases.
