How to recognise cataract symptoms

Medically reviewed by Dr. Muhammad Yahya · Last updated 2026-07-28

A cataract comes on slowly, which is why people often assume their prescription has simply changed again. The most common first sign is headlights glaring and haloing at night. Alongside that come a haze over your sight, colours looking washed out, and a prescription that keeps changing. It is not blindness — surgery restores sight.

What people notice first

Most people first think their glasses won't come clean, or that the room is badly lit. Things aren't exactly blurred, but they've lost their crispness.

Common symptoms

  • Headlights glaring at night, with haloes around them
  • A faint haze over everything
  • Colours looking washed out or yellowish
  • Sight worsening in bright sunlight
  • Needing more light to read
  • A prescription changing more than once a year
  • Double vision in one eye

Who develops it earlier

  • Older adults
  • People with diabetes
  • People who work long hours in the sun
  • Anyone with a previous eye injury
  • Long-term steroid use

Can it be prevented

Age-related cataract can't be prevented outright, but sunglasses, controlled blood sugar and avoiding tobacco can slow it.

When to see a doctor

When the loss of sight starts getting in the way of daily life — reading, driving, or recognising faces. There is no need to wait for it to “ripen”.
