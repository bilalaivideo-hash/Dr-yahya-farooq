How diabetes affects your eyes

Medically reviewed by Dr. Muhammad Yahya · Last updated 2026-07-28

Diabetes damages the fine blood vessels of the retina. The dangerous part is that early on nothing is felt — no pain, no loss of sight. That is why anyone with diabetes should have the retina checked at least once a year, even with perfect vision. Caught early, sight can be saved.

How the damage happens

Sustained high blood sugar weakens the small vessels of the retina. They begin to leak and the retina swells. In later stages, fragile new vessels form and can bleed.

Why it is noticed late

The part of the retina affected first isn't the part you use for sharp central vision. So a good deal of damage can accumulate before anything feels wrong.

Who is most at risk

  • Diabetes of several years' standing
  • Sugar that is not well controlled
  • High blood pressure as well
  • Kidney problems
  • Diabetes during pregnancy
  • Smoking

Diabetes and eyes — treated at this clinic

See treatment details

What you can do

  • Have your eyes checked at least once a year
  • Keep blood sugar and blood pressure controlled
  • Stop smoking
  • Have any sudden change in vision seen straight away

Can it be treated?

Yes, and the earlier it is found the better. In the early stages, controlling blood sugar is often enough. Later stages may need laser or other treatment.
